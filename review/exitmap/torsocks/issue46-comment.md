We think the failures described here come from a bug in `src/torsocks.py`, not from the Attacher's design.

`_Torsocket.__init__()` replaces PySocks' SOCKS5 negotiation function with `ourneg()`, which puts `(circ_id, sockname)` on the queue before it calls the original function. But `_proxy_negotiators` is a class attribute of `socks.socksocket`, so every new socket wraps the `ourneg()` of the socket before it, and the n-th socket of a probe process puts its message on the queue n times. For the second connection, the second message usually makes the Attacher attach stream `None` (`Failed to attach stream because: Unknown stream "None"`), and the stream that Tor reports next is never attached: after Tor's SocksTimeout of two minutes, the module gets `0x06: TTL expired`, unless it gave up earlier. Later even-numbered connections can fail the same way. Both approaches in the description open all their connections in one probe process. The wrapper came with 4d678bc (2020), so the rtt commit from 2016 is about something else.

The logs posted in !5 once it probed several targets per exit show the same pattern: `Attempting to attach stream None to circuit 4.`, pending attachers that only go up, and `prepare()` being called again and again with the circuit and no stream. The `TTL expired` failures posted there on 2021-01-27 are something else: that version fetched one URL per probe.

Otherwise several streams per circuit work: every connection sends its own source port with the circuit ID, so forgetting the port once its stream is attached is right. dnsresolution relies on that: its `probe()` calls `run_python_over_tor()` once per domain, and in the successful sequence posted in #61, circuit 12 carries streams 53 and 54. `resolve()` puts its message on the queue itself, so the DNS modules do not hit this bug.

!NN gives every socket its own copy of the dict (one line, plus a test); its description has a snippet that shows the duplicate messages without Tor. We could only test off the public network: against a fake Tor, and in a private network of three relays with Tor 0.4.9.11, with one `urlopen()` per `run_python_over_tor()` call. There, connection 2 failed in 27 of 27 probes on `main`, and with the fix none of the 243 connections that should have worked failed; rtt got 10 of its 10 measurements per exit instead of 1.

We could not try the full code and the targets from the description, so "after a while" may have more causes. Two we saw, with shortened timeouts only: Tor closes a circuit that has been in use for longer than `MaxCircuitDirtiness` (10 minutes by default) when no stream is open on it, e.g., while a probe pauses between two targets; and exitmap does not re-attach a stream that Tor detaches, e.g., because the exit could not resolve the name. Hence "Relates to #46" in the commit. If the fix is enough for you to close this issue, we will change that to "Closes #46".
